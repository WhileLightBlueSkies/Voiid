Clips

Clips are public. That is a decision, not an oversight.

Short video, creator profiles, follows and a camera with ten looks — built on the same account as your chats and deliberately outside their encryption. Our server stores the video, the caption and the cover in plain form, and counts every view. This page says why that had to be true, and what it costs you.

The mechanism

You cannot seal an envelope that has no address on it.

Encryption in Voiid works by sealing a message once for every device that is allowed to open it. That needs a list of recipients at the moment you post. A moment has one. A clip does not — and never will, because the point of a clip is to reach someone you have not met.

There is a second reason, and it is just as real.

Clips carry a view count, a like count and a comment count on every tile, and those numbers have to be attributed by something neither the poster nor the viewer controls — otherwise a modified client could report any number it liked. A server that cannot read a row cannot count it. We would rather write that sentence down than ship a lock icon that means nothing.

The exact scope

What our server can read, and what it still cannot.

The exception is scoped to Clips and the public identity behind them. It is not a precedent, and it does not reach sideways into anything else — those surfaces do not share a key with this one, because this one has no key.

Readable by us

  • The video file itself — stored as plain bytes, in up to three qualities
  • The caption, up to 2,200 characters
  • The cover image the grid draws instead of the video
  • Your creator handle, display name, bio, link and public avatar
  • Who follows whom, and every like and comment
  • One row per clip per viewer, which is how a view is counted once

Untouched by any of this

  • Message text, photos, voice notes and files in any chat
  • Voice and video calls, one to one and in a group
  • Live location and the pins you drop into a conversation
  • Moments, which are encrypted to an audience you named
  • Your chat profile photo — a different, encrypted object from the public avatar
Two things worth knowing before you post at all.

A public identity is not created for you at signup. There is no creator profile behind your account until the first time you try to post a clip and pick a handle — so the overwhelming majority of people, who are here to message, never get a searchable public profile they did not ask for. And your public avatar is a separate image from your chat profile photo: the encrypted one is never reused on a public surface, because that would quietly publish it.

Making one

The camera is the front door, not a picker.

Recording is where the app starts, with the library as a thumbnail inside the viewfinder — the same shape as every camera you already use. Everything after that is a description of an edit, so nothing is re-encoded until you post.

Multi-take, with speed per take

Each press and release records one take, at 0.3×, 0.5×, 1×, 2× or 3×. Takes bank up as you go and can be undone one at a time; they are joined into a single video only when you commit, so the shutter never stalls to re-mux mid-shoot.

Ten looks, live in the viewfinder

Original, Vivid, Dramatic, Mono, Noir, Fade, Chrome, Process, Transfer, Instant — the same ten, in the same order, on iPhone and Android. The preview is filtered frame by frame while the recording is written clean, so the look is applied exactly once, at export, and stays reversible until then.

Trim and cover on real frames

The editor plays video rather than showing a still, so you can judge a trim and hear the audio you are about to mute. The grid draws your cover, never the video, so you pick that frame yourself — or upload a different image entirely.

Posting does not block on the upload: the tile appears in your grid straight away and the transfer runs behind it, and leaving the screen does not kill it. If it fails, the exported files are still on your phone, so retrying re-sends those bytes instead of making you shoot or export again. The clip only becomes a row other people can see once its files have actually landed — a client that dies mid-upload leaves no broken tile in anybody’s feed.

Playback picks one of the three qualities by connection rather than switching mid-stream, and the grid never plays video at all: it draws covers, which is what makes a scroll through it affordable on mobile data.

Watching

Two feeds, both in plain time order.

Explore is every live clip, newest first. Following is a separate query over the creators you follow — a different source, not a filter laid over Explore.

There is no ranking model behind either grid. Nothing scores a clip, nothing scores you, and nothing decides what deserves the top of your screen — the order is the order things were posted in. Paging is by timestamp, so a clip posted while you are scrolling cannot make a tile repeat or vanish.

We do record that you watched something: one row per clip per viewer, written after about two seconds of watching, which is what makes a view count once however many times you rewatch it. It is not used to sort your feed. Say the two sentences together, because both are true.

A creator profile carries a handle, display name, bio, link, avatar and three counts. There is no endpoint that lists somebody’s followers — you can see the numbers, and you can see your own Following feed, but the roster itself is not exposed to anyone.

The rule that matters most

A follow is not a messaging right.

Following is one-way and needs nobody’s approval, because it grants exactly one thing: seeing content that was already public. A creator with a million followers has a million people who can watch their clips and zero extra people who can message them.

The only three ways a conversation opens

  1. 1You are both in each other’s contacts.The chat opens directly.
  2. 2They have you saved, but you do not have them.It arrives as a request you accept or decline. Having somebody’s number is not by itself permission to reach them.
  3. 3They looked you up by @username and had your 6-digit PIN.Also a request. The PIN is something you hand out yourself.

Following someone is not a fourth way.The follow graph is a separate table from the one that decides who may message you, and nothing in the messaging code reads it. Any future code that consults your followers to authorise a conversation is a bug, not a feature.

Your creator handle is not your chat username

A chat username is half of a credential — it is only useful to someone who also has your PIN. A creator handle is meant to be printed on a poster. Making them the same value would mean going public as a creator silently publishes your messaging lookup key, so they are separate fields with separate meanings.

They do share one pool of names, though: taking @ananya as a creator makes it unavailable as a chat username and the other way round, since one name meaning two different people in one app is how impersonation starts. A handful of names — admin, support, voiid and the rest — belong to nobody. Handles are lowercase, 3 to 20 characters, and changeable once every 30 days; links to your old one keep resolving to the new one until somebody else legitimately takes it.

Taking one down

Deleting a clip is not a security operation.

It removes the clip from every feed and deletes the stored files, including all three qualities. It cannot reach the people who already watched it.

The row itself survives as a tombstone rather than being erased outright, so counts and open comment lists on other people’s phones do not vanish mid-scroll — but the video, the cover and every rendition are removed from storage, and a lifecycle rule on the bucket is the backstop if that call fails.

The honest part is the second sentence: a clip was never encrypted, so anyone who watched it could have kept it, and deleting it cannot change that. The confirmation in the app says exactly this. It is the difference between unpublishing and unsaying, and only one of those is something software can do.

Our own takedowns work the same way and are reversible: a removed clip is hidden rather than destroyed, the reason is recorded, and every moderator action is written to an audit log that survives the moderator’s account. “Who removed this and why” has to have an answer, or moderation is something that happens to people rather than something anyone is accountable for.

One line, if you keep only one.

Clips are public and our server reads them. Your messages, calls, location and moments are not, and it cannot. Following someone buys nobody a way into your inbox. All three of those sentences are load-bearing.
