# Sound design — from retro synth to realistic

> **Supersedes the palette decisions in** [`../GAMES_AUDIO.md`](../GAMES_AUDIO.md). That doc's **engine** design (preloading, voice pool, ducking under calls, mute toggle, format table) stays exactly as it is — it is good and none of it changes here. What changes is **what the sounds are**.
> **Current assets:** [`apps/ios/Voiid/Voiid/Resources/GameSounds/`](../../apps/ios/Voiid/Voiid/Resources/GameSounds/) · **generator:** [`tools/gamesounds/synth.py`](../../tools/gamesounds/synth.py)

---

# 1. The change, in one line

**Today every sound in the app is synthesised** — `synth.py` renders 46 files from sine/square/noise primitives, stdlib only, no dependencies. The result is a coherent retro-arcade palette.

**The brief is to replace it with realistic, recorded audio**: a real crowd during cricket, a real bat-on-ball crack for a wicket, real chalk for Tic Tac Toe.

This is the right call, and it is worth being precise about why: retro bleeps say *"this is a small game inside a chat app."* Recorded audio says *"this is a real game."* Hand Cricket in particular is a game about atmosphere — a stadium is half the experience, and a square wave cannot supply one.

---

# 2. What this costs — read before starting

The synth approach had three properties that recorded audio does not. Each needs a deliberate answer, or the change makes the app worse in ways that only show up after shipping.

| Property | Synth today | Recorded | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Size** | 46 files, tiny, procedural | Real recordings are 10-100× larger. A crowd loop is the worst case | §7 — budget, encode as AAC, cap the crowd loop length |
| **Consistency** | Every sound shares one synthesis vocabulary, so they automatically sit together | Sourced clips come from different rooms, mics and levels and will clash badly | §6 — mastering pass is **mandatory**, not optional |
| **Licensing** | Generated by us, no question | Every clip needs a licence that permits commercial redistribution in an app | §8 — non-negotiable, and the thing most likely to bite later |

`synth.py` should be **kept, not deleted**. It stays useful for UI ticks and as the fallback for anything a recording can't do better (see §5.5).

---

# 3. The core rule you asked for: one catch sound, everywhere

> *"catch sound should be same in each sound for all games"*

**A single shared "catch" sound plays at the corresponding moment in every game.** This is the most important decision in the doc, so it gets stated as a rule:

> **`catch.wav` is one file, played unmodified, at the moment a player's attempt is intercepted or ended by the opponent.**

| Game | The "catch" moment |
|---|---|
| Hand Cricket | Wicket falls — the pick was matched |
| Tic Tac Toe | Your winning threat gets blocked |
| Rock Paper Scissors | Your throw is countered (you lose the round) |
| Snake | You are killed by another snake (not by the border) |

**Why this is worth doing.** A shared sound across four games builds a vocabulary: the player learns in one game what it means, and it carries. It is the audio equivalent of a brand. Four unrelated "you lost that one" sounds teach nothing and make the games feel like four unrelated apps stapled to a tab.

**Implementation rules:**

- **One file, one name.** `catch.wav`. Not `catch_cricket` / `catch_snake` — the moment it forks, the vocabulary is gone.
- **Play it unmodified.** No per-game pitch offset. `GameAudio`'s varispeed exists for Snake's `eat` variation; do not use it here. Identical means identical.
- **Layer, never replace.** Cricket's wicket = `catch` **+** crowd reaction (§4.1). Snake's kill = `catch` **+** the impact tail. The shared sound is the constant; the game supplies the context around it.
- **Gain may vary per game** to sit correctly in each mix. That is mastering, not identity.

**What it should sound like:** a leather-on-palm slap with a short room tail. ~180 ms. Physical, dry, decisive — the sound of something being *stopped*. It must survive being heard 30 times in a Snake match without becoming irritating, which rules out anything bright or ringing.

# 4. Per-game sound design

## 4.1 Hand Cricket — the crowd is the point

> *"i want crowd sound during cricket and wicket hitting sound should sound like that"*

Currently: `wicket.wav`, `four.wav`, `six.wav`/`six.m4a`, `runs_1..6.wav`, `innings.wav`, `pick.wav` — all synthesised, all dry, no crowd anywhere.

### The crowd bed — the single biggest upgrade in the whole app

A **continuous, looping stadium ambience** underneath the entire match, whose intensity tracks the state of the game. This is what makes it feel like cricket rather than arithmetic.

| Layer | File | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Base murmur | `crowd_base.m4a` | Loops for the whole match. Seamless, 20-30 s, low gain (~0.18). This is atmosphere, not an event |
| Tension swell | — | The **same file**, gain ramped up to ~0.35 as the chase tightens. No new asset |
| Reactions | `crowd_cheer.m4a`, `crowd_gasp.m4a`, `crowd_groan.m4a`, `crowd_roar.m4a` | One-shots layered **over** the base, never replacing it |

**Drive the intensity from state the engine already sends.** `target`, `ballsBowled`, `overs` and `wickets` are all serialized. Required run rate rising → crowd rises. This costs one file and a gain curve, and it is the difference between ambience and drama:

```
required rate < 6      → base gain 0.18   (calm)
required rate 6-12     → base gain 0.26   (engaged)
required rate > 12     → base gain 0.35   (tense)
final over             → +0.05 and slightly brighter EQ
```

**Loop it properly.** A crowd loop with an audible seam is worse than no crowd — the ear locks onto the repeat within about three cycles. Needs a real crossfade at the loop point, and ≥20 s so the period is long enough not to be recognised. `GameAudio` already has a dedicated `loopVoice` outside the round-robin pool (built for Snake's `boost_loop`); the crowd bed uses that same path.

### Wicket — the sound you specifically asked for

> *"wicket hitting sound should sound like that"*

A wicket is **three sounds in a stack**, not one:

```
0 ms    catch.wav              ← THE SHARED SOUND (§3). Leather on palm
0 ms    wicket_timber.wav      ← ball striking stumps: wood crack + bail rattle
120 ms  crowd_roar.m4a         ← the reaction, delayed so the impact lands first
```

That 120 ms delay is what sells it. A real crowd reacts *after* the event — simultaneous playback reads as one mushy noise and loses the impact entirely.

`wicket_timber` is the "hitting" sound in your brief: a hard, woody crack with the distinctive light rattle of bails falling. Dry and close-mic'd, because the room is supplied by the crowd layer sitting under it.

### The rest of cricket

| Event | Sound | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pick committed | `pick.wav` | Keep it small and dry — a UI confirm, not a cricket sound. Under the crowd bed |
| Reveal | `reveal.wav` | Short woody tick, the beat both picks land on |
| Runs 1-3 | `bat_soft.wav` | Bat-on-ball contact, gentle. Replaces the tonal `runs_1..3` |
| Four | `bat_crack.wav` + `crowd_cheer` | Real willow crack |
| Six | `bat_crack.wav` (louder) + `crowd_roar` | The biggest moment in the game — give it the biggest reaction |
| Dot ball (0) | `bat_block.wav` | Dull, muted thud. Deliberately anticlimactic — a dot ball *should* deflate |
| Innings break | `crowd_applause.m4a` | Scattered applause, tapers over ~2 s |
| Match end | `crowd_roar` (win) / `crowd_groan` (loss) | The crowd delivers the verdict |

**Note on run sounds:** replacing tonal `runs_1..6` with one `bat_soft` plus gain/pitch variation loses the pitch-encodes-run-count information the current design has. That is an acceptable trade — the on-screen number carries it, and six distinct musical pitches is the most "retro video game" thing in the current palette.

## 4.2 Snake

Snake's current palette is the one place retro genuinely fits — it is an abstract neon arcade game, not a physical one. **Do not make Snake realistic.** There is no real-world referent for "a glowing snake eats a pellet," and chasing one produces worse sound, not better.

**Change only three things:**

| Event | Change |
|---|---|
| **Kill** | Layer `catch.wav` (§3) under the existing `kill.wav`. This is the vocabulary rule; Snake is not exempt |
| **Death by border** | Distinct from a kill — no `catch`. You were not caught, you crashed. A low physical impact plus a sub drop |
| **Eat** | Keep the four synthesised variants and the varispeed. This is working and is the most-triggered sound in the app |

`boost_loop`, `border_warn`, `spawn` all stay as they are.

## 4.3 Tic Tac Toe — chalk

> *"add chalk sound when writing, cross type chalk and round type chalk sound"*

Currently `mark_x.wav` and `mark_o.wav` are synthesised blips. Replace with **real chalk on slate**, and — as you specified — the two marks sound genuinely different, because drawing them is a different physical action.

| Sound | File | Character | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **X** | `chalk_x.wav` | **Two strokes.** Sharp, angular, with a tiny gap between them — you can hear it is two lines. Percussive attack on each, slight scrape tail | ~260 ms |
| **O** | `chalk_o.wav` | **One continuous stroke.** Smoother, sustained, a single curved scrape with no internal break. Rounder, less attack | ~300 ms |

This distinction is the whole point of the request: **X sounds like two strikes, O sounds like one sweep.** A player learns to hear whose turn resolved without looking, which is a real accessibility win as well as a texture one.

**Variation matters here.** Chalk is never identical twice, and Tic Tac Toe fires this sound up to nine times in ~30 seconds — the most repetition-exposed sound in the game. Ship **3 variants each** (`chalk_x_1..3`, `chalk_o_1..3`), chosen at random, with the existing varispeed applied at ±4%. Without this it becomes machine-like by move four.

### The winning line

Per [`TICTACTOE_WIN_LINE.md`](./TICTACTOE_WIN_LINE.md) §2.2, the line is a **drawn stroke**, so its sound must be too:

- `chalk_line.wav` — one long, confident, continuous scrape. ~340 ms, **matched exactly to the stroke animation duration.** Starts on the same frame the stroke begins (at the 120 ms hold mark, not on the winning mark).
- Louder and lower than the mark sounds — this is the decisive gesture, drawn harder.
- **The sound must not outlast the animation.** A scrape still going after the line has stopped moving is the kind of mismatch that reads as broken without the player knowing why.

### Everything else in Tic Tac Toe

| Event | Sound |
|---|---|
| Invalid move | `chalk_stub.wav` — a short dull chalk tap that goes nowhere. Physical "that didn't take" |
| Draw | `chalk_erase.wav` — a duster wiping the slate. This gives the draw its own identity, which [`TICTACTOE.md`](./TICTACTOE.md) §2.2 flags as missing |
| Win | `chalk_line` + a soft success tone |
| Block (your threat stopped) | `catch.wav` (§3) |

**Board material must be consistent.** Chalk implies slate. If the board renders as glass or neon, either the sound or the visual is lying. Worth checking against the actual board styling before recording — this is the kind of mismatch nobody can name but everybody feels.

## 4.4 Rock Paper Scissors

The game is one dramatic beat ([`RPS.md`](./RPS.md) §2.3), and the sounds should be **hand sounds** — cloth, skin, air:

| Event | Sound |
|---|---|
| Countdown (3 beats) | `hand_pump.wav` — the fist-pump whoosh, ×3, rising slightly in pitch |
| Reveal | `hand_reveal.wav` — a short cloth/air snap. Both throws land on this one frame |
| Round win | Bright confirm |
| Round loss | `catch.wav` (§3) — you were countered |
| Round tie | Flat, neutral, deliberately unsatisfying |
| Match win / loss | Existing stingers, re-recorded to match the new palette |

# 5. Sourcing

## 5.1 What to get

| Priority | Sound | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `catch.wav` | Shared by all four games (§3). Every other decision depends on its character |
| 2 | `crowd_base` + 4 reactions | Biggest single upgrade in the app |
| 3 | `wicket_timber` | The specific sound in the brief |
| 4 | `chalk_x` ×3, `chalk_o` ×3, `chalk_line` | The other specific request |
| 5 | `bat_soft`, `bat_crack`, `bat_block` | Completes cricket |
| 6 | RPS hand sounds | Smallest game, least exposure |

## 5.2 Where

- **Freesound.org** — filter strictly to **CC0**. Best source for chalk, wood impacts and crowd. Quality varies enormously; audition on phone speakers, not headphones.
- **A commercial library** (Soundsnap, A Sound Effect, Boom) — one small licence covers everything here and removes the attribution burden. **Recommended** given §8.
- **Record it.** Chalk on slate and a ball hitting stumps are both trivially recordable with a phone in a quiet room, and they will beat a generic library clip because they will be exactly the length and character specified above. The crowd is the only thing genuinely worth licensing.

## 5.3 Crowd loops specifically

The hardest asset here. Requirements: **≥20 s**, **seamlessly loopable**, **no identifiable individual voices** (a distinct shout becomes a metronome on repeat), **no language** (it ships internationally), and recorded at distance so it sits *under* the game rather than in front of it.

## 5.4 Auditioning

**Test on a phone speaker, at low volume, in a noisy room.** That is where this app is used. Sounds chosen on headphones consistently fail there — sub-bass vanishes entirely and anything above ~4 kHz becomes harsh.

## 5.5 Keep the synth

`synth.py` stays for `tap`, `sheet_open`, `sheet_close`, `error`, `mark_invalid`, `match_found`, `invite_arrive`, `countdown_*` and all of Snake's abstract palette. Recorded audio is right for *physical* events; UI is not a physical event, and a recorded UI tick would be a downgrade. Update the file's docstring so the split is explicit and nobody later assumes the whole palette is procedural.

# 6. Mastering — mandatory

Sourced clips will not sit together. This pass is what makes 40 files from 12 sources sound like one game.

1. **Normalise to a shared loudness target**, not to peak. **−16 LUFS integrated** for one-shots, **−26 LUFS** for the crowd bed (it lives underneath everything). Peak-normalising is what makes one sound feel twice as loud as another at the same numeric level.
2. **High-pass everything at 80 Hz** except deliberate impacts (`wicket_timber`, Snake death). Phone speakers reproduce nothing below that; the energy only eats headroom and causes distortion.
3. **Trim silence hard at both ends.** Leading silence is perceived latency — and this app already has `GameAudio` tuned for sub-frame trigger latency, which a 40 ms lead-in would silently throw away.
4. **Match the room.** Cricket sounds share one space (stadium). Chalk sounds share another (small dry room). Do not mix a reverberant chalk with a dry one — this is the single most common tell of assembled-from-sources audio.
5. **Cap one-shots at 800 ms** except crowd reactions and match-end stingers. Long tails overlap the next event and turn a fast game to mud.
6. **Mono.** Everything. The engine is wired mono end to end — [`GameAudio.swift`](../../apps/ios/Voiid/Voiid/DesignSystem/GameAudio.swift) documents that a stereo buffer on a mono-wired bus is a **hard AVAudioEngine crash** (an ObjC exception `try?` cannot catch — it was the Snake-screen crash). **A stereo asset will crash the app.** This is the highest-risk line in this document.

---

# 7. Format and budget

Follow [`../GAMES_AUDIO.md`](../GAMES_AUDIO.md) §6, with one amendment for size:

| Class | Format | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| One-shots < 500 ms | 16-bit mono WAV 44.1 kHz | Instant, no decode |
| One-shots > 500 ms | AAC `.m4a` | Already the existing rule |
| **Crowd loops** | **AAC `.m4a`, mono, 64 kbps** | New. A 30 s WAV is ~2.6 MB; at 64 kbps mono it is ~240 KB |

**Budget: keep the total game audio payload under 4 MB per platform.** Currently it is a few hundred KB. Realistic audio will multiply that, and the crowd bed is the whole risk — one over-long stereo WAV blows the budget on its own.

**Verify decode cost for the crowd loop.** `GameAudio` preloads into PCM buffers; a 30 s AAC decodes to ~2.6 MB of PCM in memory regardless of file size. That is acceptable for one file, but it must be released on match exit (`release(for:)` already exists — make sure cricket's crowd bed is registered with it).

---

# 8. Licensing — do this before recording anything else

Every sourced clip needs a licence permitting **commercial use and redistribution inside a compiled application**. CC-BY requires attribution the app currently has nowhere to put; CC0 and commercial libraries do not.

**Keep `tools/gamesounds/LICENSES.md`** recording, per file: source URL, licence, author, and date retrieved. Do it as files are added, not afterwards — reconstructing provenance for 40 clips later is miserable and is how apps end up shipping something they cannot defend.

This is the one item here that is a real liability rather than a quality issue.

---

# 9. Build order

1. **Licence decision** (§8) — blocks all sourcing.
2. **`catch.wav`** (§3) — everything else is designed around its character.
3. **Chalk set** (§4.3) — self-contained, smallest game, proves the whole pipeline end to end on one screen.
4. **Win-line sound + animation together** ([`TICTACTOE_WIN_LINE.md`](./TICTACTOE_WIN_LINE.md)) — they are one feature and must be timed against each other.
5. **Crowd bed + intensity curve** (§4.1) — the big one. Ship the bed and the gain curve before the reaction one-shots; the bed alone transforms the game.
6. **Wicket stack** (§4.1) — `catch` + `wicket_timber` + delayed roar.
7. **Remaining cricket** — bat contacts, innings break, match end.
8. **Snake `catch` layer** (§4.2) — one line.
9. **RPS hand sounds** (§4.4).
10. **Mastering pass over everything** (§6) — do this **last, over the complete set**, not per-file as you go. Consistency can only be judged against the full palette.

Steps 2-4 are a complete, shippable slice: one shared sound and one game fully converted. If the chalk lands, the approach is proven and the rest is execution.
